I've continued to study and write a blog, but recently I haven't been able to write much due to work and personal schedules.  
There are many parts of the research areas that cannot be disclosed, so I am unable to write about them.  
   
In this article, I want to say something to the juniors at my school and to those who have just started studying security.  
   
With the development of AI, CTFs these days have become competitions that discuss how to use AI better and gain FLAG, rather than competing with hacking skills. What can I say, the romance of discussing vulnerabilities and exploit methods with team members while analyzing binaries and googling them seems to no longer exist...  
\[ Even as I say this, I placed 1st in INCOGNITO CTF before AI was activated, and I always placed in the top 20 in Hacktheon, although I did not make it to the finals. Other competitions were similar. I really participated and enjoyed it, but CTF using the current AI was difficult because I am not yet good at using AI... \]

  
Now that I am working in the field, I have seen the resumes of many job applicants and acquaintances who have applied to our company, and have heard various opinions from the evaluators.   
So, in this post, I would like to talk about this briefly.  
   
These days, the evaluation of CTF award performance has been lowered significantly. Previously, having a record of awards was a means of certifying one's ability, but now it seems to be moving towards showing a lot of interest rather than evaluating one's ability.

In fact, I felt it even more because I intuitively observed that the juniors were using AI to win awards at the CTF I recently participated in with my juniors.   
So, I'm not saying that using AI is bad. Rather, I welcome it.  
To be precise, we welcome cases where AI is not just used for purposes such as CTF, but also has a portfolio that involves conducting research by bringing AI into personal research.  
In the end, I think what is important is not whether AI was used, but what kind of thinking and results were created using AI.  
The level of simply automating problem solving has now become an area accessible to anyone, and in the future, how to connect the insights gained in the process to one's own research or development will become more important.  
In the past, the CTF award itself was an indicator of one's skills, but now, based on that experience, what analysis was written, what tools were created, and what research topics were expanded seem to be becoming stronger evaluation factors.  
   
So, I would like to ask a question to those who are just starting to study security.

**Now that AI is being replaced, how will you conduct research on security concepts/technologies???**   
**Should we just rely on AI??**  
 

When I look at my juniors and acquaintances, there are many cases where they leave the research of concepts to AI even when learning basic concepts, do not study technology, and just click MCP.  
If you are really going to leave everything to AI, wouldn't there be a need for companies to use AI and hire new people?  
   
Recently, a junior colleague asked me a question, and I answered it like this.  
Let's say A, who has expertise, and B, who knows nothing, are working on a project at the same time with the same goal. 

![](/assets/images/tistory/tistory-9ad8ad342007/001.png)

   
Because A has an understanding of and experience using basic concepts and technologies, he can directly design the pipeline for the overall procedure. You will also be able to configure detailed prompts about the rules or directions needed for the AI ​​to perform its tasks.  
As a result, you are more likely to create results that go beyond simply using AI and are more in line with your goals.  
On the other hand, B will try to rely on AI from beginning to end, and even if AI presents incorrect information due to hallucination, it is highly likely that it will accept it as is without verifying it.  
In the end, even if the results are similar on the surface, there are significant differences in the process of analyzing and verifying the problem.  
So, in the future, I think that the basics of understanding and verifying AI results and the ability to lead them in the desired direction will become more important than simply “Can AI be used?”  
   
The introduction was long, but the bottom line is that I think I need to continue studying.  
Of course, you don't have to go as deep as before...  
(For your information, I tend to pursue studying by doing things myself rather than using AI until each concept and technology is properly established.)  
   
Rather than being able to use AI, it would be better to develop **the basic skills to understand and verify AI results**.  
 

\+ Modified 26.08.20) When utilizing AI, there are times when a request is made to execute a specific command or code. In addition, various technical terms are used when organizing data in formats such as md. In this case, the difference between having basic knowledge and not having it has a big impact on the direction of the work and the quality of the results. In order to make good use of AI, I think studying each field is not an option, but a necessity!

  
I wrote this lightly, but this is just my opinion based on AI development, so each person can study in the direction they think.
